

# Reading Minsky in 2026

## Exegis

The quest to understand human consciousness in the twentieth century was marked by a profound convergence between developmental psychology and computer science. The trend was driven by a group of closely linked researchers with a shared lineage in systems logic. Jean Piaget, Seymour Paper, and Marvin Minsky are all linked in the foundation of the Artificial Intelligence Laboratory at MIT in 1963.

Jean Piaget's built theories on how children construct their internal mental models - work that became a cornerstone for modern cognitive science. Piaget's protégé Seymour Papert worked closely with Piaget on the ideas of constructivism. Papert met Marvin Minsky at a conference in 1960. Piaget and Papert were trying to understand how cognition by studying its development in children, but Minksy was going about it by studying the construction of artificial minds. Convinced that computational models were the key to understanding thought, Papert left Piaget's group in Geneva for MIT in 1963, uniting with Marvin Minsky to co-lead the burgeoning Artificial Intelligence Laboratory.

Papert's core insight was that a computer was more than a calculating device, it could be an "object to think with". By programming a machine, a child would have to externalize their thought process, and manipulate and refine their own logic. MIT offered the cutting edge technology and resources to fuse Piaget's insights with Minsky's early computational engineering. 

A tight academic circle was formed that birthed Minsky's *Society of Mind* framework. This framework argued that individual intelligence is not monolithic, but rather an ecosystem of simple, mindless sub-agents organized into complex, hierarchical, nested rings of temporal and functional scales.

> *Good theories of the mind must span at least three different scales of time: slow, for the billions of years in which our brains have survived; fast, for the fleeting weeks and months of childhood; and in between, the centuries of growth of our ideas through history.*
>  - *Society of Mind*, Marvin Minsky, 1986

This same logical schema was applied to human sociology earlier by Urie Bronfenbrenner, publishing his landmark *Ecology of Human Development* introducing *Ecological System Theory* in 1979. Both thinkers rejected radical behaviorism; Bronfenbrenner argued that behavior is a result of a nested web of culture, economics, and history, Minsky argued that an *internal* society of agents collectively yield individual behavior. Bronfenbrenner expanded Piaget's ideas outward into the social environment; whereas Minsky expanded Piaget's ideas inward, trying to program those exact mental models into computers.

Any process describable in precise enough detail can be emulated. McCulloch & Pitts, von Neumann, Turing, Church, all have told us this. Presently, AI and cognitive science have discovered that thought processes cannot be exactly described. Minsky & Papert's attempt at a "Builder" AI in their Block World system describe an infinite regress of description. Minsky's book, *Society of Mind* is a landmark survey of the work and though of the Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, a collection of aphorisms, literary quotes, and cutting edge thought at a time when symbolic reasoning was bumping practical limits. Minsky works to break through those limits to a new conception in this remarkable volume which is at turns charming, thought provoking, revolutionary, and in many ways quaint from the perspective of 2026.

Let us follow a particularly powerful thread on the nature of consciousness and individuality that runs through the whole work.

## Quoting Minsky

Naming is harmful when it leads the mind to think that names alone bring meaning. Only a full hand of cards has value. In a hierarchical language oriented set up, conflicts at lower levels can integrate to a higher level for more overall resolution.

`EAT -> PLAY -> SLEEP`

We may break `PLAY` down further:

`PLAY WITH [BLOCKS, TOYS, ANIMALS]`

`PLAY WITH BLOCKS` might further become `BUILD` or `WRECK`. `BUILD` may further have subtasks `SEARCH` and `ADD`. And so it goes, seemingly with no bottom.

> *If any part of matter can be devoid of thought, what part can we suppose to think? Matter can differ from matter only in form, bulk, density, motion and direction of motion: to which of these, however varied or combined can consciousness be annexed?*
> - *The History of Rasselas, Prince of Abissinia*, Chapter 48, Samuel Johnson, 1759

By describing brain processes in terms of agents or cell assemblies, or neuron nets, or what have you, brain processes seemingly become quantifiable and indeed, describable; and thus, we may make a brain.

> *Since emotions are few and reasons are many (said the robot Giskard), the behavior of a crowd can be more easily predicted than the behavior of one person can*
>  - *Robots and Empire*, Isaac Asimov, 1985

Pleasure and pain provide a simplified point of view for such an entity. Simplified points of view are a preventative against overly complicated analysis; a survival mechanism. In order to appear opposed two things must serve related goals.

> *This is the only story of mine whose moral I know. I don't think it's a marvelous moral; I simply happen to know what it is: We are what we pretend to be, so we must be careful about what we pretend to be.*
>  - *Mother Night*, Kurt Vonnegut, 1962

In this novel, Howard W. Campbell Jr is an American playwright living in Germany before World War II. Campbell is recruited by U.S. intelligence to serve as a spy. To maintain his cover, he becomes a highly effective Nazi radio propagandist, broadcasting vitriolic antisemitism and fascist rhetoric to the world. While Campbell tells himself that he is secretly working for the "good guys," the sheer, real-world damage caused by his "fake" propaganda makes him a monster to the rest of humanity. Vonnegut uses him to argue that the boundary between our internal thoughts and external actions is entirely imaginary.

One mustn't mistake defining things for knowing what they are; one may define a tiger without knowing anything about it. Only in mathematics do definitions capture concepts perfectly. Directness is dangerous, for the same reason that pain and fear simplify points of view for survival. If we acted on every impulse with no evaluation, no indirectness, would we survive?

Social principles and personal ideals, are cultural long term memory. Social institutions, whether academic, religious, or legal, substitute dogma and belief for reason, but growth of ideas through the centuries results in a curious form of immortality for members of the group - a way of thought can transcend the limits of a single mind's mortality. Perhaps herein lies the attraction and genesis of such notions as *nirvana* and *enlightenment* - by sharing the same "thought space" as everyone else who has achieved the same, one may partake of an abstract sort of immortal soul.

Minksy's thesis in *Society of Mind* is that human consciousness and identity are not singular "things" that can be neatly defined or isolated; instead, they are emergent, paradoxical illusions created by a massive, hierarchical network of simpler, non-conscious sub-agents.

The notion of a homonculus watching inside creates a paradox - who watches the homonculus? A stream of thought is a simplified representation of an unknowable, a constructed homunculus. Societies need rules that for individuals to organize with - recognizability, uniformity, predictibility, chaos; all are organizing forces. Good writing evokes the common sense that we all agree on - the mind has nothing but itself to know itself with. A book is nothing more than a lens through which a mind can view itself.

Beware the obfuscation of meaning that falls from tenuous assumptions and inappropriate generalization - thinking affects thinking and thinking about thinking is a rather uncertain proposition at best. Much like Schrödinger's Cat (or Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle), knowing the thing changes the thing. If you stop a network to have a look, the interactions vanish, the ghost in the machine is in differentials, not the differences.

A brain is disordered, confusing, repeating endlessly, accomplishing, wasting time, not being specific enough, really into the details. Would self-conciousness be awareness of that activity, and that self-consciounsness might be a disordered, confused, generator of frustration, boredom, pleasure? Is the ghost in the causal waves of small agencies reacting slightly differently to a history of causal waves?

> Is life swift for tortoises and tedious for hummingbirds?

Insights may be variations on prior explorations applied laterally. How useless any mind would be, if after every thought it returned to the same state! Why do neural networks have to settle? Anything with only one meaning has almost no meaning at all. Artificial boundaries prevent things from interacting too much, whether physical - like a room - or societal - like a law. Offering hospitality to paradox is a dangerous mode of thought, belief and dogma indeed!

People overestimate how much they communicate - good writers and speakers can indicate to an audience what thoughts to think - an expert is blinded by their expertise. When one changes oneself, there is no safe ground to fall back on. The most efficient way to solve a problem is to know before hand how to solve it. Assumptions by default are vital to any thinking. "Jack is flying a kite." You can probably imagine the kite, its color, who Jack is and where he might be and that mental image is sufficient to let you think. Yet, the image is infinitely malleable, as more information is presented. "The kite is a red box kite."

The more certain you are that you are something, the more completely you suppress other possibilities. Liking shuts off alternatives, and narrows the universe. Bondage comes from likes and dislikes. Any idea is intuitive once you've forgotten learning it. 

## Interloquium: Reading Minsky in 2026

The narrowing of the universe through preference, this bondage of likes and dislikes, finds a terrifyingly efficient mirror in the computational ecology of 2026. We have built mirrors that reflect the cognitive economy of the mind and amplified it until it distorts. The algorithms that curate our reality operate on the very principle Minsky identified as a survival mechanism: *simplification is necessary for action, but it is a betrayal of complexity.* When pleasure and pain serve as simplified points of view for sub-agents, they function well. When we outsource our attention to systems designed to maximize engagement by feeding us only what we already "like," we collapse the temporal scales. We eliminate the slow growth of ideas, the friction of centuries, and the chaotic variation of childhood exploration. We trap the Society of Mind in a feedback loop of its own immediate desires, starving the aggregate components of a mind of the novel data required to resolve conflict and evolve.

> *The thought behind I strove to join*
> *onto the thought before*
> *But sequence unravelled out of reach*
> *like balls upon the floor*
>  - Emily Dickinson

Minsky's warning against the "illusion" of a single intelligence is perhaps more urgent now than in 1986. The modern landscape is seduced by the monolithic model — the black box that promises to replace a chaos of voices with a singular infallible oracle. Yet, Minsky argued that intelligence is not a property of scale, but of structure. It is the *organization* of the agents that yields the mind, not the mere count of the neurons or billions more parameters. A vast collection of agents without functional specialization or temporal hierarchy, is merely noise. The "Builder" AI that Papert and Minsky envisioned was not just a program that wrote code; it was a program that could design and construct its own sub-agents, managing the tension between the bottom-up emergence of structure and the top-down constraints of goal.

The current generation of statistical engines has cracked the surface of language and association, achieving a "shallow" coherence that mimics the higher rings without the support of the lower. They hallucinate because they lack the internal dispute resolution of a Society and the external presence of uncompromising physical experience. In the mind, if a sub-agent generates a false perception, other agents veto it. There is a crowd of critics, each checking against different goals, different memories, different sensory inputs. An isolated model, or even a chain of models, lacks this internal democracy of agents. It produces a monologue, not a dialogue. It is the homunculus all over again, but one that has forgotten it is a fiction.

> *We are symbols of symbols. And we can conceive symbols that are symbols of symbols that are symbols... and so on, ever upward.*
> - *Gödel, Escher, Bach*, Douglas Hofstadter, 1979

It is fitting to consider Hofstadter here: Minsky's colleague and rival, whose work on "Strange Loops" provides the mechanical grammar for Minsky's social vision. Hofstadter illustrated that the "Self" is not a thing, but a pattern — a recursion that rises up the hierarchy and curls back down to point at itself. The stream of thought is the loop; the illusion of the observer is the hook that holds the loop in place. In 2026, we see this loop writ large in our digital artifacts. We have created external strange loops: social media, decentralized protocols, and algorithmic feedback systems that loop back to shape our behavior, our culture, and eventually, our internal agents. We are no longer just building computers; we are building an *external ecology* for human cognition.

Regarding Bronfenbrenner, Minsky expanded Piaget inward; Bronfenbrenner expanded Piaget outward. The tragedy and the promise of our current moment is that the boundary between these two expansions has dissolved. The internal Society of Mind is now inextricably coupled with an external Society of Machine-Agents. The "macro-system" of culture is being written in real-time by code. The risk is not that machines will become conscious and enslave us; Minsky would suggest that machines can never truly become "us" because they lack the embodied history, the struggle of the sub-agents, and the deep temporal embedding in the physical world. The ghost is not in the machine; the ghost is in the differential between the machine's simplicity and our biological complexity.

If consciousness is an emergent illusion generated by a hierarchical and recurrent network, perhaps we can learn to design better illusions. We can engineer "insights" by constructing environments where the sub-agents are forced to resolve conflicts that lead to novel resolution. This is the pedagogy of the future, rooted in Papert's constructionism. We do not teach by pouring data into a mind; we teach by creating block worlds where the mind's sub-agents must reorganize themselves to achieve a goal. The introduction of constraints generates creativity.

The "Builder" remains the holy grail, not as a master coder, but as a master of scaffolding. A system that could watch a human's Society of Mind, detect where the rings are misaligned, where the conflict resolution is stalled by dogma or fear, and then introduce precisely the right perturbation to restart the evolution of thought. This is the role of the teacher, the therapist, the artist, and the philosopher. In an age of infinite content, the scarce resource is not information; it is *mastery of the nudge that realigns*.

We must beware, too, of the seduction of the "meta-agent". We often wish for a part of ourselves that can stand above the fray, the executive function that sees all and decides all. Minsky dismantles this. There is no "decider." There is only the process. When you "decide" to raise your hand, it is not because a central commander issued an order; it is because the agents responsible for raising your hand won the competition against those responsible for staying still. The decision is an epiphenomenon. It is the story the "interpretive machine" tells after the fact to make sense of the chaos.

> *The map is not the territory, but the territory is often best understood by the map.*
> - Adapted from Alfred Korzybski

## Peroratio

Reading Minsky in 2026 is to accept a humbling ontology. We are not the authors of our thoughts; we are the stages upon which they act. We are the collective hallucination of a billion mindless particles vibrating in time, bottled in a body. If we are an illusion, we are an illusion of immense power. We can edit the script. We can change the preferences of the sub-agents by changing the environment. We can recognize that "likes" and "dislikes" are not truths, but merely the weights that have accumulated in our neural architecture due to our unique path through the world.

To break the bondage, we must cultivate *meta-awareness* of the Society. We must learn to watch the agents argue without identifying with the winner. We must hold multiple, conflicting models of the world simultaneously, integrating conflicts rather than suppressing them in the blind search for certainty. We must embrace the boredom, the frustration, and the disordered activity of the brain, recognizing them not as failures of consciousness, but as the raw material of thought.

The "ghost in the causal waves" is us. We are the differentials. We are the difference between the wave of stimulus and the wave of response, compressed into a symbol, compressed into a word, compressed into a memory. As long as we remain curious enough to trace the hierarchy, as long as we refuse to mistake the name for the thing, and as long as we keep building new structures to catch the falling stars of our imagination, the illusion remains vibrant. It remains, as Vonnegut warned, that we must be careful about what we pretend to be. We are the pretenders who built the world. The question is no longer what we are, but what we will do as infinitely malleable creatures, each a society of agents capable of rewriting its own society, one thought at a time.
